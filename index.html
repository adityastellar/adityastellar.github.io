01

Registration-based indicators for detecting abusive activity

PUBP 8803 · Prof. Sergio Caltagirone · Spring 2026

Execution of the complete intelligence cycle for an assigned external customer, encompassing requirements definition, collection strategy, analysis and a written dissemination product. The engagement addressed the customer's objective of reducing abusive account registrations on a public intelligence platform without restricting access for the analysts, journalists and researchers who constitute its user base.

Intelligence cycleOSINTRecorded FutureMITRE ATT&CKConfidence grading

Analysis of thirty domains spanning disposable services, mainstream providers, confirmed abuse and typosquatted infrastructure established that no attribute observable at registration reliably predicts subsequent abuse. Vendor risk scores were found to measure exposure within threat reporting rather than intent, while structural indicators such as typosquatting and homograph similarity proved the most dependable signal available at registration, as they rely on string comparison rather than accumulated telemetry. The final report was issued at TLP:AMBER with MITRE ATT&CK mappings and seven recommendations.

Three domains, identical vendor risk score. Which should registration policy block?

The report concluded that registration-time attributes cannot reliably predict abuse. Each answer explains why.

C   score 24  ·  mail records none  ·  malware hosting

TPM backed remote attestation

CS 6238 Secure Computer Systems · Spring 2026

A Python attestation service verifying virtual machine instances through nonce-based challenge and response and PCR-bound sealing policies, requiring a host to demonstrate its measured boot state before trust is extended.

TPM 2.0PCRsSealing policiesOAuth 2.0 / PKCEOktaPython

A modified bootloader completes boot successfully. The register value diverges from the sealing policy, so a compromise at boot level obtains a sealed secret rather than usable key material.

Cybersecurity Intern, VAPT and Governance

January to May 2025

A seventeen-week engagement divided between offensive security testing and governance, reporting to the Head of Governance, Risk and Compliance. Testing covered more than fifty web applications, APIs, network hosts and DNS assets against OWASP methodology, together with mobile assessments and internal network penetration testing across five business units, producing over forty reports supported by reproducible proofs of concept.

OWASPBurp SuiteNessusISO/IEC 42001DPDP ActSEBI CSCRF

Security testing

Findings ranged from injection, JWT and OTP bypass vulnerabilities at the application layer to default credentials and end-of-life services within the internal network. A password reset endpoint was found to implement no rate limiting: one thousand requests returned one thousand HTTP 200 responses and generated one thousand emails. A domain password spray identified weak credentials clustered by department, indicating shared onboarding practice rather than isolated user error.

Governance and compliance

Governance responsibilities included drafting an enterprise AI governance policy aligned to ISO/IEC 42001 and cross-checked against the EU AI Act, assessing the existing privacy policy against India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, and expanding a SEBI CSCRF compliance dashboard from fifteen to thirty-four tracked parameters.

Process and documentation

I established a central API inventory with an associated severity model, initiated an internal vulnerability-tracking database modelled on National Vulnerability Database conventions, and authored team methodology documentation covering grey box testing, red teaming and the quarterly SEBI assessment cycle.

The engagement demonstrated that remediation outcomes depend as much on the quality of reporting as on detection, as findings are acted upon when the recipient can reproduce them quickly and without ambiguity.

An analysis of Cl0p's exploitation of CVE-2023-34362 structured through the Diamond Model, including the socio-political and technology meta-features, followed by an argument for locating the policy response at the national level and a set of proposed measures covering secure development, software liability and supply chain transparency.

INTA 6103 · NOV 2025

What Explains Variation in National Cyber Doctrines Across Democracies?

A structured comparison of the United States, Germany and India examining threat environment, domestic institutions and alliance structure as explanations for doctrinal divergence, with explicit falsification conditions stated for each hypothesis. Germany provides disconfirming evidence for the threat-environment hypothesis, supporting domestic institutions as the dominant mediating variable.

PUBP 8823 · DEC 2025

Why Pakistan Disrupts While China Infiltrates

An examination of why Pakistan and China employ structurally different forms of subversion against India, arguing that rivalry type rather than regime type or material capability accounts for the divergence. The argument is developed through a six-stage causal mechanism and process tracing on the Pulwama attack and on RedEcho's intrusions into Indian power infrastructure.

CS 6268 · APR 2026

Manipulating the Help Desk: Group Behaviour in the Twitter and MGM Breaches

A comparative analysis of the 2020 Twitter and 2023 MGM Resorts compromises, both initiated through pretextual telephone contact rather than technical exploitation. The paper argues that group-level psychological processes rather than individual error account for their success, and that verification procedures require structural redesign rather than additional awareness training.
