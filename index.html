Registration-Based Indicators for Detecting Abusive Activity

PUBP 8803 · Prof. Sergio Caltagirone · Spring 2026

Execution of the complete intelligence cycle for an assigned external customer, encompassing requirements definition, collection strategy, analysis and a written dissemination product. The engagement addressed the customer’s objective of reducing abusive account registrations on a public intelligence platform without restricting access for the analysts, journalists and researchers who constitute its user base.

Intelligence cycleOSINTRecorded FutureMITRE ATT&CKConfidence grading

Analysis of thirty domains spanning disposable services, mainstream providers, confirmed abuse and typosquatted infrastructure established that no attribute observable at registration reliably predicts subsequent abuse. Vendor risk scores were found to measure exposure within threat reporting rather than intent, while structural indicators such as typosquatting and homograph similarity proved the most dependable signal available at registration, as they rely on string comparison rather than accumulated telemetry. The final report was issued at TLP:AMBER with MITRE ATT&CK mappings and seven recommendations.

TPM-Backed Remote Attestation and Identity Protocols

CS 6238 · Dr. Vijay Madisetti · Spring 2026

A Python attestation service verifying virtual machine instances through nonce-based challenge and response and PCR-bound sealing policies, requiring a host to demonstrate its measured boot state before trust is extended.

TPM 2.0PCRsSealing policiesBell-LaPadulaBibaClark-WilsonOAuth 2.0 / PKCEOktaPython

A modified bootloader completes boot successfully. The register value diverges from the sealing policy, so a compromise at boot level obtains a sealed secret rather than usable key material.

The same coursework addressed formal security models: Bell-LaPadula for confidentiality with PCR-sealed secrets, Biba for integrity with measurement-gated writes, and Clark-Wilson with TPM-signed ledger and IVP audit, all enforced through TPM 2.0 hardware primitives.

The identity protocols component implemented the OAuth 2.0 Authorization Code flow with PKCE against Okta, then selectively disabled PKCE, state and nonce validation to demonstrate authorisation code interception, cross-site request forgery and replay against the implementation.

Privacy Impact Assessment of AI-Assisted Legal Technology

CS/PUBP 6726 · Prof. Peter Swire · Fall 2025

A privacy impact assessment of LuaLegal, an AI legal meeting assistant, conducted using the NIST Privacy Framework and Department of Justice assessment templates and evaluated against the Electronic Communications Privacy Act, the FTC Fair Information Practice Principles and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework.

Privacy impact assessmentNIST Privacy FrameworkNIST AI RMFECPAFTC FIPPsISO/IEC 27701SOC 2

The assessment covered a three-stage processing pipeline (ingestion, processing, output storage) operating on AWS with TLS 1.3 in transit and AES-256 at rest, with four AI sub-processors (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Cloud AI, AWS) operating under zero data retention terms.

Eight privacy risks were scored for likelihood and impact: unauthorised access, AI model retention, third-party integration, metadata leakage, insider threat, cross-border transfer, model inference attacks and data aggregation. Ten mitigating controls were mapped across the pipeline, and five recommendations were delivered: publish audit summaries, per-participant timestamped consent verification, vendor prompt-injection and model-retention testing, self-service deletion and export, and annual PIA review.

The assessment addressed the divergence between contractual retention commitments and the consent actually obtained from meeting participants, and drew on NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, SP 800-88r1, SP 800-61, ISO/IEC 27701 and SOC 2 alongside the primary frameworks.
